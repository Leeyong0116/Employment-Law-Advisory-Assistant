Management of Employee Termination

1. What is the meaning of termination of employment?

Termination of employment refers to the ending of a service contract due to company closure or redundancy of employees. Redundancy may occur for several reasons, such as company restructuring, reduction in production, company mergers, technological changes, company takeovers, and others. This does not include types of contract termination caused by disciplinary actions or dismissal of employees.

2. What steps should an employer take to avoid employee termination?

When a situation of employee redundancy arises, the employer shall first take appropriate measures to avoid employee termination as recommended in the Procedures for Industrial Harmony, namely:

2.1 Freeze the hiring of new employees EXCEPT for critical areas

2.2 Limit Overtime Work

2.3 Limit work on weekly rest days and public holidays;

2.4 Reduce the number of working days per week or decrease the number of shifts;

2.5 Reduce daily working hours;

2.6 Conduct retraining programs for employees;

2.7 Identify alternative work and transfer employees to other departments or roles within the same company;

2.8 Implement temporary layoffs (lay-off) for example, in the form of a temporary closure (temporary shut down) by offering reasonable wages and assisting them in finding temporary employment elsewhere until operations can resume. If the employer implements a temporary layoff, the duration of the layoff must be reported to the Sabah Labour Department for monitoring purposes, to determine whether the employees are re-employed, offered Voluntary Separation Scheme (VSS), or permanently terminated in the future; and

2.9 Reduce employees’ salaries (Pay-Cut) applied fairly at all levels, can also be implemented as a last resort after other cost-saving measures have been carried out.

The measures in items 2.7, 2.8, and 2.9 shall be implemented with the written consent of the employees or the trade union representing them.

3. What steps should an employer take if employee termination becomes unavoidable?

If employee termination needs to be carried out, the employer shall endeavor to implement the following measures to mitigate the negative impact on the affected employees:

3.1 Notify and hold discussions with the employees or the trade union representing them as soon as possible regarding the impending employee termination;

3.2 Offer a voluntary termination/retirement scheme with the best possible compensation package;

3.3 Pay termination compensation or benefits to eligible employees;

3.4 Grant retirement to employees who are above the normal retirement age;

3.5 Provide a notice of termination of employment as stipulated in the employment contract. If there is no provision in the contract regarding this, the employer shall give notice as specified under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) as follows;

a) 4 weeks’ notice if the employee has worked for less than 2 years;

b) 6 weeks’ notice if the employee has worked for 2 years but less than 5 years; or

c) 8 weeks’ notice if the employee has worked for 5 years or more.

3.6 Assist employees in finding employment elsewhere before termination is carried out, in cooperation with the Sabah Labour Department under the Ministry of Human Resources;

3.7 Implement employee termination in phases over an extended period;

3.8 Apply the ‘FWFO’ (Foreign Worker – First Out) principle within the same job category when terminating employees, meaning foreign workers are terminated before local employees; and

3.9 Apply the ‘LIFO’ (Last-In-First-Out) principle if the termination involves local employees within the same job category. However, the employer may also implement employee termination based on specific criteria after consulting and obtaining consent from the employees or the trade union representing them.

4. What are the employer’s responsibilities in reporting employee termination actions and related measures to the Sabah Labour Department (JTK Sabah)?

4.1 Employers are required to report to the nearest Sabah Labour Department at least 30 days before any of the actions listed below are carried out, using the Termination Form, according to the type of termination the employer intends to implement:

4.1.1 Employee Termination (Retrenchment);

4.1.2 Voluntary Employee Termination (Voluntary Separation);

4.1.3 Temporary Layoff (Lay-Off); or

4.1.4 Reduction of Employees’ Salary (Pay-Cut).

4.2 The Termination Form can be obtained free of charge from any Sabah Labour Office.

5. What are the rights of employees affected by termination?

Employees who are terminated are entitled to the minimum termination benefits if they are covered under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) or a Collective Agreement.

5.1 Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67)
In general, employees covered under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) and affected by termination are entitled to claim the following statutory benefits:

5.1.1 Wages in lieu of notice

Employees who are terminated without being given sufficient notice of termination may claim wages in lieu of notice (indemnity). The wages in lieu of notice must be paid no later than the last day of the terminated employment contract.

5.1.2 Termination benefits

Payment of termination benefits shall follow the employment contract, but shall not be less than the rates prescribed under the Labour Rules (Sabah), as follows:

a) 10 days’ wages for each year of service if the employee has served less than 2 years;

b) 15 days’ wages for each year of service if the employee has served for 2 years but less than 5 years; or

c) 20 days’ wages for each year of service if the employee has served 5 years or more, calculated on a pro-rata basis for incomplete years, rounded to the nearest full month.

5.1.3 Employees are entitled to receive written details of the total termination benefits and the method of calculation. Termination benefits must be paid no later than 7 days from the date the employee’s service is terminated.

5.1.4 Payment in lieu of unused annual leave, if any.

5.1.5 Remaining Salary

5.1.6 Employees affected by termination may refer to the nearest Labour Department for further information

5.2 Industrial Relations Act 1967

5.2.1 If the employees affected by termination are union members and have signed a Collective Agreement, they are entitled to the following:

i) Compensation as specified in the Collective Agreement; or

ii) Reinstatement under Section 20 of the Industrial Relations Act 1967, if the employee is dissatisfied with the termination action taken by the employer.

5.2.2 For employees not covered under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) or a Collective Agreement, they are subject to the terms and conditions of their employment contract or the employer’s discretion regarding the amount of compensation to be received.

5.2.3 Employees may seek further advice from the nearest Industrial Relations Department office to their workplace.

6. What should employees do if they are terminated?

The steps that should be taken by employees who are terminated are:

i. Report the proposed or actual termination action taken by their employer to the nearest Labour Office.

ii. Register with the nearest Social Security Organization (SOCSO/PERKESO) office.

iii. Ensure that termination compensation or benefits have been received (if entitled).

Private Employment Agency (PEA)

1. What are the categories of private employment agency (PEA) licenses under the Private Employment Agencies Act 1981 (Act 246)?

There are three types of licenses, as follows: 

  • License A: For job placement of local job seekers within Malaysia
  • License B: For job placement of local job seekers both within and outside Malaysia, and for foreign domestic workers within Malaysia
  • License C: For job placement of local job seekers within and outside Malaysia, and for non-citizen workers including foreign domestic workers within Malaysia
2. What are the requirements for applying for a PEA (Private Employment Agency) license?
  1. A corporate body incorporated under the Companies Act 2016 (Act 777);
  2. Has a minimum paid-up capital as follows:
    • License A: RM50,000;
    • License B: RM100,000;
    • License C: RM250,000.
  3. At least 51% of the company’s total shares are held by Malaysian citizens;
  4. The company’s directors are:
    • Malaysian
    • Not an undischarged bankrupt; and
    • Not convicted of offenses related to human trafficking and forced labor;
  5. Have suitable premises as determined by the Director General;
  6. Have a company name prefixed with the words “Agensi Pekerjaan” (Employment Agency); and
  7. Any other requirements as prescribed by the Director General.
3. Why do agents placing non-citizen (foreign) workers need to obtain a PEA license?

One of the purposes of introducing the PEA license is to regulate the placement of non-citizen (foreign) workers. Accordingly, any agent without a license under this Act is not permitted to carry out placement activities of foreign workers to any employer. This is to prevent an oversupply of foreign workers without actual employers, avoid exploitation, and curb human trafficking activities.

Only PEAs with a Category C license are allowed to place non-citizen workers with employers throughout Malaysia.

4. How to apply for a PEA (Private Employment Agency) license?

PEA license applications can be made by completing the relevant form along with supporting documents as specified in the Checklist.

The form and checklist can be downloaded from this website.

All application forms and checklists related to license renewal, category change, branch opening, premises relocation, and license replacement can also be downloaded via the same link.

The form and supporting documents according to the checklist, along with the processing fee, must be submitted to the Labour Office nearest to the agency’s premises.

5. What actions can be taken against individuals/agencies operating without a license?
Any agency or individual engaging in unlicensed agency activities commits an offense under Section 7 of Act 246 and, if convicted, may be fined up to RM200,000, or imprisoned for a term not exceeding three years, or both. 
6. What is the purpose of introducing an identity document?

Every PEA employee involved in recruitment activities must have an identity document. The purpose of this identity document is to prove that the employee is a legitimate PEA worker. It also helps prevent impersonation, exploitation, and fraud.

7. How can one apply for the identity document?

To apply for the identity document, complete the application form and submit the supporting documents as listed in the Checklist. These documents must be submitted to the Labour Office nearest to the PEA premises, along with the processing fee for the identity document.

8. Can a PEA (Private Employment Agency) open a branch?

A PEA can open a branch as follows:

  • License A: Can only apply License A 
  • License B: Can only apply License A or License B
  • License C: Can apply License A, B, and C
9. Under what circumstances can a PEA license be suspended or revoked?

A PEA license can be suspended or revoked under the following circumstances:

  1. Has violated any provision of the Act and Regulation
  2. Has not complied with the conditions imposed by the Director General
  3. Has been convicted of an offence under this Act or the Director has been detained under any written law relating to anti-trafficking in persons and forced labour.
  4. Has been wound up or dissolved.
  5. Has not complied with any written direction issued by the Director General under this Act.
  6. Has submitted false information in the process of obtaining a license.
  7. Has misused the licence for purposes contrary to any other law, or;
  8. Has ceased to carry out recruiting activities.
10. Can a PEA (Private Employment Agency) surrender or transfer its license to another party?

A PEA cannot surrender or transfer its license to another party except with prior written approval from the Director General.

Surrendering or transferring a license without prior written approval from the Director General is an offense punishable by a fine not exceeding RM100,000, imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years, or both.

11. Can a PEA (Private Employment Agency) lease or rent out its license to another party?

A PEA is not allowed to lease or rent out the license granted under this Act to any person.

Leasing or renting out a license is an offense punishable by a fine not exceeding RM100,000, imprisonment for a term not exceeding 2 years, or both.

12. Can a PEA (Private Employment Agency) operate at a location/premises different from that stated in its license?

A PEA cannot operate at a location/premises different from that stated in its license. A PEA wishing to change its business location/premises must first obtain written approval from the Director General.

An application to change or relocate PEA premises can be made by submitting the form and supporting documents to the nearest Labour Office. The PEA may only operate at the new location/premises after approval has been granted.

13. What is the amount of the license fee?

The license fee is RM500.

 

14. What is a security deposit?

A security deposit is a guarantee in the form of cash or a financial guarantee issued by a licensed bank under the Financial Services Act 2013, which must be deposited into a Government account.

The purpose of the security deposit is to serve as collateral. If the PEA fails to fulfill its responsibilities to job seekers, non-citizen workers, or employers, the security deposit may be used.

Wages / Salary / Minimum Wage

1. Who are the employees protected under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67)?

The Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) protects employees under the following categories:

  1. Any person who enters into an employment contract
  2. Employees earning a salary exceeding RM4,000 per month.
  3. Manual workers (with no salary limit).
2. When is the deadline for wage payment?
No later than the seventh (7th) day after the end of the wage period.
3. When must all payments be made if an employee is terminated by the employer?
No later than the day the contract is terminated or the following day, provided it is not a public holiday.
4. When must all payments be made if an employee resigns from work?
  • If an employee resigns with sufficient notice, wages must be paid on the day they leave employment.
  • If an employee gives insufficient notice, the employer must pay wages no later than the seventh (7th) day after the end of the wage period.
5. Is wages required to be paid if an employee is absent due to attending court?
  • Wages are paid to employees attending court if the court justifies that their presence is necessary to assist in upholding public justice.
6. Can wage deductions be made if the employer overpaid the employee’s wages in the past five (5) months?
  • Wages may be deducted only for overpayments made within the three (3) months prior due to the employer’s error.
7. Can deductions be made for the purpose of paying for uniforms or accommodation rent?
  • Not allowed EXCEPT with written approval from the Director of the Labour Department (JTK).
8. Can wage payments be made by the employer via bank transfer or cheque?
  • Wages must be paid through a financial institution, and if paid by cheque, prior written consent from the employee must be obtained.
9. What is meant by the ordinary rate of wages?
    • The ordinary rate of wages for monthly-salaried employees is calculated using the following formula:

    Monthly Wage / 26

    • The ordinary rate of wages for weekly-salaried employees is calculated using the following formula:

    Weekly Wage / 6

    For an estimated calculation, click on the Labour Calculator in the Help Center menu.

General Question

Termination
1. Is the employer required to notify the Labour Department (JTK) if they intend to terminate an employee?

The employer must report to the Labour Department (JTK). at the nearest office by completing the termination form at least 30 days before the employee’s termination is carried out.

2. What is Voluntary Separation or the Voluntary Separation Scheme (VSS)?

Voluntary separation is a scheme in which employees are allowed to voluntarily terminate their employment contract with compensation offered by the employer..

3. My employer informed us that the business will be temporarily shut down and employees will not be paid during this period. Is my employer allowed to do this?

The employer can do so and this action is considered as Temporary Employment Termination or lay-off. However, several conditions must be complied with by the employer. According to Regulation 5(1), Work Regulations (Termination Benefits and Temporary Lay-Off) 1980, a temporary termination or lay-off occurs when the employer does not provide work or pay wages for at least 12 ordinary working days within a consecutive 4-week period. If either of these situations occurs, the employer must pay temporary lay-off benefits to the employees. However, if the employer does not resume operations and the employment contract is terminated or not renewed, the employer must pay termination benefits. A temporary lay-off does not apply if the employer fails to meet the conditions under Regulation 5(1).

4. During the shutdown period, are monthly-salaried employees entitled to wages?

Yes. The court has ruled that monthly-salaried employees who are not provided work during a shutdown are entitled to full wages. The employer must pay wages based on the employment contract for employees on a monthly salary.

5. My employer decided to close business operations for one week due to a public holiday and asked employees to use their annual leave during this period. Can this be considered a temporary lay-off?

According to Regulation 5(1) of the Work Regulations (Termination Benefits and Temporary Lay-Off) 1980, rest days, public holidays, sick leave, maternity leave, annual leave, leave allowed under written law, and any leave applied for and approved by the employer, cannot be considered as a temporary lay-off or lay-off.

Terms of Service
1. What must an employer provide when employing a worker?

The employer must maintain a register containing certain information such as personal details, Terms & Conditions details and Allowance and wages details , retrived from every salary

2. Is the employer required to provide a written employment contract?

The employer must provide an employment contract and to provide one copy to the employee. as soon as the employee commences employment, if the contract exceed 1 month and within a certain time.

3. Is it permissible to change the terms of service?

The terms and conditions of service may be amended subject to the mutual consent of both the employer and the employee. provided that it does not contravene any provision under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67). and Other applicable laws

4. Do terms and conditions that are less favorable to the employee apply?

Any term of service that provides lesser benefits to the employee is invalid and has no legal effect if it does not follow the terms and condition as prescribed under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67)..

5. What are the employer’s responsibilities if they intend to terminate an employee’s service?
  1. The employer’s primary responsibility is to provide employment notice sufficient to the employee whose service is to be terminated.
  2. Pay in lieu of notice if the employer does not provide sufficient notice.
  3. Termination benefits for employee that worked 12 months continuous service with the same employer.
  4. Paying back yearly holiday salary in which workers can claim.
  5. Other payment which the employee is entitled to receive, such as outstanding wages.
  6. The employer’s responsibility is to repatriate the employee to their home country, or home state, or original working place for employee whose service has been terminated
6. What is the notice period that must be given by the employer or employee? If it is not specified in the employment contract, the period is as follows:
  1. Less than (2) years of services : 4 weeks notice or 4 weeks’ pay in lieu of notice.
  2. (2) years but less than (5) years of services : 6 weeks notice or 6 weeks’ pay in lieu of notice..
  3. (5) years Services and above : 8 Weeks notice or 8 weeks’ pay in lieu of notice..
7. Under what circumstances can an employer terminate an employee’s contract without notice?

An employer may terminate employment under the following circumstances:

  1. Intentional breach of the employment contract.
  2. Misconduct that is inconsistent with compliance with the terms of service.
8. What other actions can an employer take if an employee commits an offense, aside from terminating the employment contract without notice?

Employers may demote position from emplloyee or execute a reasonable minor disciplinary action including suspension without pay for a period not exceeding two (2) weeks’ wages when theres an internal investigation

Female Employee
1. How many days of paid maternity leave is a female employee entitled to receive?

98 days continuously.

2. Is a female employee eligible to receive maternity pay/allowance if she already has five children?

Eligibility to receive maternity pay/allowance is up to five surviving children

3. How much maternity allowance am I entitled to?

The amount of maternity allowance that female employees are entitled to receive from their employers is based on the normal salary rate.

4. When can maternity leave be taken?

Maternity leave may be taken up to thirty (30) days before confinement or from the day immediately following confinement. However, an employee may be required to commence her maternity leave at any time within fourteen (14) days before confinement if certified by a medical officer or a registered medical practitioner appointed by the employer that she is unfit to perform her duties due to her pregnancy.

5. What are the eligibility requirements for a female employee to receive maternity leave and allowance?

To be eligible for maternity leave and maternity allowance, an employee must fulfill the following conditions:

  • She must give birth after at least twenty-two (22) weeks of pregnancy.
  • She was employed at any time within the four (4) months prior to giving birth.
6. Through this amendment to the act, female employees who are on maternity leave are allowed to come to work early before the end of the 98-day maternity leave period. Does this mean that employers can also instruct female employees who are on maternity leave to come to work early before the end of the 98-day maternity leave period?

Not allowed except with the female employee's own application and with the employer's consent and certified as healthy to work by a registered medical practitioner.

7. If a female employee who is eligible for maternity allowance agrees to return to work before the end of her maternity leave, does the employer need to provide additional payment in addition to the maternity allowance?

No. However, employers are encouraged to provide additional payments.

8. Can an employer terminate the employment of a female employee who is pregnant?

Through the amendment to this act, employers cannot terminate the services of female employees who are pregnant or have an illness arising from pregnancy except in the following circumstances:

(i) Termination due to willful breach of contract of service under section 13(2);

(ii) Termination due to misconduct under section 14(1); and

(iii) Termination due to closure of the employer's business.

Paternitiy Leave
1. How much paternity leave entitlement is given to male employees under this amendment to the Ordinance?

Paternity leave eligibility is 7 consecutive days including rest days and public holidays and the eligibility date begins on the date the employee's wife gives birth.

2. When does paternity leave begin and can the leave be granted intermittently?

No, paternity leave must be granted continuously for 7 days.

3. What are the conditions that allow a male employee to get 7 days of paid paternity leave?

Conditions to be eligible for 7 days paid paternity leave:

(i) Male employees must be married to their spouse;

(ii) The male employee must have worked for at least 12 months with the same employer before commencing the leave;

(iii) The employee must inform his employer at least 30 days before his wife gives birth or as soon as possible after the birth;

(iv) Paid paternity leave entitlement is limited to 5 births regardless of the number of wives.

4. If a wife suffers a miscarriage after 22 weeks of pregnancy, is a male employee entitled to paternity leave if he has complied with all other conditions?

Yes. This is because the definition of “confinement” under section 2 means the birth of a child that occurs after 22 weeks of pregnancy regardless of whether the child is alive or dead.

5. If an employee has been granted paternity leave 5 times by the employer before this amendment comes into effect, is the employee still entitled to the paternity leave prescribed under section 105EA?

Employees are still entitled to paternity leave because paternity leave granted before the effective date of this amendment is not taken into account in calculating the maximum limit of 5 births.

6. Mr. Johari was employed by Syarikat Harap Jaya for 12 months prior to the effective date of the amendment. When his wife gives birth after the effective date of the amendment, is Mr. Johari entitled to paternity leave after complying with all other conditions set out in the amendment?

Yes. Mr. Johari is entitled to paternity leave because section 105EA(3) as amended stipulates that married male employees who have been employed by the same employer for at least 12 months immediately before commencing the leave are entitled to paternity leave.

7. Are Rest Days and Public Holidays taken into account in calculating the 7-day paternity leave?

Yes, because paternity leave is calculated continuously from the date the wife gives birth, including rest days and public holidays.

8. If a female employee suffers a miscarriage after 22 weeks of pregnancy, is her husband eligible for 7 days of paid paternity leave if all other conditions have been met?

Eligible because the definition of childbirth under section 2 of this Ordinance is the birth of a child that occurs after at least 22 weeks of pregnancy regardless of whether the child is alive or dead.

Working Hours
1. Why, through this amendment to the Ordinance, have working hours been reduced from 48 hours a week to 45 hours a week?

The working hours per week have been reduced to be in line with the recommendations of the International Labour Organization through Reduction of Hours of Work Recommendation, 1962 (No. 116) which recommends that countries that practice a 48-hour work week take steps to reduce working hours.

2. Can an employer reduce an employee's salary due to the implementation of working hours that do not exceed 45 hours a week?

It is not possible since the employee's salary is an agreed condition in the service contract.

3. What are the employer's responsibilities when the amendment involving the reduction of weekly working hours is enforced?

The employer must make amendments to the working hours in the service contract so that it is in line with the amendments to this Ordinance and provide a copy of the amendments to the employee concerned.

4. Is the working time 45 hours a week including breaks?

Excluding break times provided that the employee is free to use his/her time and movement during the break.

5. To implement the reduction in working hours from 48 hours a week to 45 hours a week, can the employer reduce the working day from 6 days a week to 5 days a week, and increase the working hours from 8 hours a day to 9 hours a day?

Yes. The proviso under section 105(1) allows the number of working hours per day to be extended to 9 by mutual agreement if the number of working hours on one or more days in a week is less than 8.

6. If an employer is currently practicing a 6-day work week and reduces the working hours per day from 8 hours to 7.5 hours after the amendment comes into effect, is the calculation of the "hourly rate of pay" divided by 7.5?

Yes, the calculation of the “hourly rate of pay” is divided by 7.5 because the normal working hours have changed. The definition of “hourly rate of pay” means the normal salary divided by the normal working hours.

7. For employees who are paid based on an hourly rate, can the employer pay a salary of RM8.17 per hour because the amendment to the Ordinance has not yet been enforced?

No. The wage rate paid by employers to employees is regulated by the minimum wage order, where the Minimum Wage Order 2024 sets the minimum wage rate for employees paid based on an hourly wage rate at RM8.72 per hour.

8. How can employers implement a reduction in working hours from 48 hours to 45 hours?

Reducing working hours from 48 hours to 45 hours can be implemented through the following methods:

Example 1: Reduce working hours every day of the week with an earlier return time.

Medical Leave
1. Why are sick leave and hospitalization sick leave entitlements separated through the amendment to this act?

The amendment to this Ordinance has separated the entitlement to sick leave and hospitalisation sick leave where employees will get higher paid sick leave from 60 days per year to 74 to 82 days per year (depending on length of service). The purpose of this amendment is to enable employees to get sufficient treatment and recovery period.

Dismissal
1. What are the basic conditions regarding termination benefits?

The service contract is terminated by the employer under a contract that continues for not less than 12 months.

2. What are the reasons why employers are required to pay termination benefits?
Termination benefits must be paid when the employer terminates the service contract for any reason unless:-
 
  • The service contract is terminated because the employee has reached retirement age.
  • The employee was terminated under Section 14(1) of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) after due investigation was conducted.
  • The employee has resigned except under Section 13(2) or 14(3) of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67).
  • Employees whose service contracts are renewed or re-employed on the condition that the terms and conditions of service are the same or better.
3. What is the rate of severance pay?
The total severance pay cannot be less than the following:
 
  • Less than two (2) years of service – 10 days' wages for each year of service.
  • More than two (2) years but less than five (5) years – 15 days' wages for each year of service.
  • Five (5) years and above of service – 20 days' wages per year of service.

For an estimated count, click on Labor Calculator under the Help Center menu.

4. When are termination benefits mandatory to be paid?

Termination benefits must be paid no later than the seventh (7) day after the termination date.

5. Is the employer obliged to provide details of the payment of termination benefits?

Employers are required to prepare a statement containing details regarding the calculation of termination benefits.

6. Under what circumstances does temporary layoff occur?

It occurs when the employer is unable to provide the employee with work where wages are paid based on the work provided in the service contract for not less than 12 days in any four-week period.

7. What is the rate of temporary layoff pay?

The amount of temporary layoff payment cannot be less than the following:

  • Less than two years of service – 10 days' wages for each year of service.
  • More than two years but less than five years – 15 days' wages for each year of service.
  • Five years and above of service – 20 days' wages per year of service.
Penalty
1. What is the punishment if an employer is late in paying wages?

An employer who is late in paying wages is committing an offence under Section 130J(c) of Chapter XIVA and may be fined not more than ten thousand ringgit.

2. What is the penalty if an employer makes deductions from wages contrary to Section 114?

An employer who makes deductions from wages is committing an offence under Section 130J(e) and may be fined not more than ten thousand ringgit.

3. What is the penalty if the employer fails to pay sick leave pay?

An employer who fails to pay sick leave pay is committing an offence under Section 130K(5) and upon conviction the court may order the employer to pay the amount of sick leave pay as if a fine were imposed under the same Section.

Labour Court
1. What is the punishment if the employer fails to comply with the Labor Court order?

An employer who fails to comply with an order or decision of the Labour Court is guilty of an offence under section 130C(a) and if convicted may be liable to a fine not exceeding RM50,000.00 and if the offence continues, may be liable to a fine not exceeding RM1,000.00 per day. For an employer who fails to comply with an order or direction of the Labour Court regarding discrimination in employment, is guilty of an offence under section 19A and if convicted may be liable to the same fine as above.

2. What is the Labor Court?

The Labour Court refers to the inquiry proceedings where the Director or an authorized officer may hear and determine complaints relating to wages and other payments under a contract of service, statutory payments provided for under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) and the National Wage Negotiating Council Act 2011. In addition, the Labour Court may also hear and determine any complaints relating to discrimination in employment provided for under section 19A.

3. What are the objectives of the Labour Court?

The objective is to resolve disputes simply, cheaply and quickly.

4. How long does it take to resolve a case in the Labour Court?

Generally, the target set for case resolution is set at no more than three (3) months depending on the complexity of the law involved.

5. Who can file a complaint at the Labour Court?

Workers who fall under the definition of “employee” under the First Schedule of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) can file a complaint at the Labour Court.

6. What is the maximum number of claims in the Labor Court?

The Labour Court has no limit on the number of claims.

7. How much does it cost to file a complaint at the Labour Court?

Filing a complaint is free of charge. Costs involved such as transportation and others are borne by the respective parties.

8. Can lawyers appear to represent parties in Labour Court proceedings?

Representation is not provided for under the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67). However, the officer handling the case may exercise discretion to allow any party in the case being handled to be represented. Any party in the case who wishes to be represented must submit a power of attorney form before the case commences.

9. If an order is not complied with, what further action can be taken?

If the order made fails to be complied with by the party ordered, the order may be endorsed and enforced in the relevant Sessions Court or Magistrate as if the order was an order made by the relevant court. In addition, failure to comply with the order made is also an offence under section 19A, or section 130C(a), as the case may be.

10. If you are not satisfied with the decision or order made, can it be appealed?

An appeal against a decision or order may be made to the High Court within fourteen (14) days from the date the decision or order was made.

11. How is the appeal process done?

The appeal process is made by filing a notice of appeal, an appeal deposit of RM1,000.00 in the form of a money order or bank draft. All the matters stated must be submitted to the Manpower Office where the order or decision was made within fourteen (14) days from the date the decision or order was made.

Employment of Non-Resident Workers in Sabah
1. Is it an offence for someone to employ non-resident workers without an employment licence?

Yes, it is a mistake if someone is employing non-resident workers without obtaining an employment license first (Section 119 of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67)). Failure by any party to obtain a license to employ non-resident workers from the Director is an offence under Section 130L of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) and if convicted, may be punished with fine not exceeding RM50,000 or imprisoned for not more than one (1) year or both at the same time.

2. Is it an offence if someone forges, alters or tampers with a Non-Resident Worker Employment License that has been issued?

Yes, any party who commits falsification, alteration or any form of tampering with a Non-Resident Worker Employment License that has been issued is committing an offence and is punishable by fine not exceeding RM50,000 or imprisonment for not more than one (1) year or both at the same time.

3. Who is defined as a “Non-Resident Worker”?

“Non-Resident Worker"is any party working in Sabah that is non-permanent resident status in Sabah according to provisions Section 71 Immigration Act 1959 / 1963. There are 3 types of non-resident Sabah workers which is non-Malaysian citizen workers, workers are from Sarawak and Penisula Malaysia.

4. How do I obtain a License to Employ Non-Resident Workers?

Employers who wish to employ non-resident workers are required to apply for an employment license from Department of Labour Sabah. Applications will only be accepted and processed for approval by employers who intend to employ employee according to the definition below Section 2 Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67).

Regardless of the Type of Job

"Any party, regardless of their occupation, who is bound by a contract of service"

5. What sectors are allowed to apply for Approval in Principle (AP) in Sabah?

Provided is a list of sectors allowed to apply for AP and subsequently issued a license to employ non-resident workers in Sabah: –

  • Manufacturing
  • Agriculture
  • Construction
  • Transportation
  • Services – AP applications from this sector are only considered for restaurant operators, coffee shops, reflexology service centers and laundries.
6. What steps do employers need to take to obtain an employment license?

First, the employer must apply Approval in Principle Letter (AP) and can then apply for a New License Under AP via Integrated Non-Resident Employee Management System (eNORES)

7. What steps do employers need to take before an AP application can be submitted?

First of all, the employer needs to register via Integrated Non-Resident Employee Management System (eNORES).

8. What should employers take into account when managing applications for Licenses to Employ Non-Resident Workers, Trade Officers, Temporary Employment Passes and Domestic Helpers?

Some things that employers need to pay attention to are as follows:

1. Ensure all application documents are complete and accurately filled in the system eNORES.

2. That the salary offered to the non-resident employee applied for is equivalent to the position and not less than the minimum wage rate in force.

3. Non-resident workers applied for must have qualifications or experience no less than those advertised.

4. In addition, employers must comply with the provisions of applicable labor laws.

5. Comply with the applicable non-resident employment policy.

9. How long is the license valid for and the period allowed for a non-resident worker to work in Sabah?

The validity period of a license may be allowed for two (2) year EXCEPT for the Construction and Reflexology sectors which is only for a period of one year. A non-resident worker is allowed to work in Sarawak for a maximum period of up to six (6) year EXCEPT for the Oil Palm Plantation sector is allowed for a maximum period of up to ten (10) years.

10. What is the maximum age limit for the position of General Worker for a new license application under AP?

Maximum age limit for General Employee position is 45 years for new license applications under AP.

11. Is it an obligation for employers to provide residential facilities to non-resident employees employed at their Place of Employment?

Yes, it is the employer's duty to provide residential facilities for non-resident workers who are paid at their place of work.

12. Are employers required to submit a “Check Out Memo” document for non-resident workers who have been repatriated to their country of origin if the employer wishes to employ additional non-resident workers to replace the workers who have returned?

Yes, the employer must submit documents "Check Out Memo" as proof of repatriation to the worker's country of origin to enable AP to be issued to recruit new workers.

13. Is it mandatory for employers to submit the Non-Resident Employee Employment Information Statement Form 119A to the Manpower Office?

Yes, employers who employ non-resident workers are required to submit Form 119A to the nearest Labour Office within (14) days employment of non-resident workers.

14. Is it an offence if the employer fails to submit information on Form 119A to the Manpower Office?

Yes, failure to do so constitutes an offence under Section 130L of the Sabah Labour Ordinance (Sabah Chapter 67) and may be punished if convicted of an offense fine not exceeding RM10,000 or imprisonment for 6 months or both at the same time.

15. How often does the employer need to submit information in Form 119A to the Manpower Office?

The employer is required to complete Form 119A and sent to the the nearest Labour Office or update in eNORES

Minimum Salary
1. What is the definition of minimum salary?

The definition of minimum wage under section 2, National Wage Bargaining Council Act 2011 [Act 732] is the basic salary only, excluding any allowances or other payments.

2. When is the effective date of PGM 2024?

On 1 February 2025:

(a) Employer that employs 5 workers or more; and

(b) Employer who runs a professional activity regardless of the number of employees who are employed by the employer, who are classified under Malaysian Occupational Classification Standards (MASCO) as officially published by the Ministry of Human Resources.

(c) On 1 August 2025:

Applies to all employees currently employed by the employer.

4. Who is entitled to receive?

PGM applies to all employees Including non-citizens in the private sector except domestic service and apprenticeship contracts.

5. Why are foreign workers not exempt from receiving the minimum wage?

The government has determined that the PGM covers all workers including foreign/non-citizen workers based on the following reasons:

i. Minimum wage policies must not discriminate against an employee on grounds of nationality, in line with ILO Convention No.100: Equal Remuneration which was ratified by Malaysia in 1997;


ii) It is contrary to section 69F of the Employment Act 1955 [Act 265], section 118B of the Sabah Labour Ordinance [Chapter 67] or section 119B of the Sarawak Labour Ordinance [Chapter 76] which do not allow any form of discrimination between local workers and foreign workers;

6. What are the legal implications when the Minimum Wage Order 2024 is gazetted?

The legal implications when PGM 2024 is gazetted are:

i). PGM 2022 is void. However, any legal proceedings, prosecution or investigation made under PGM 2022 shall continue until completed; and

ii) All service contracts and collective agreements must take into account all provisions in the PGM 2024.

7. Can employers pay a base salary lower than the minimum wage if their employees agree?

ABSOLUTELY NOT. This is against the law.

8. What are the penalties for employers who do not pay the minimum wage?

Employers found guilty of not paying the minimum wage to employees may be subject to the following penalties:-

First Offense

General Penalty

Fine not exceeding RM10,000 for each offence if no penalty is provided.

Penalties for Continuing Offences

Daily fine not exceeding RM1,000 for offences that continue after conviction.

Penalties for Repeating Offences

Fine not exceeding RM20,000 or imprisonment not exceeding 5 years.

9. Is the minimum wage contrary to the practice of the Productivity-Linked Wage System (PLWS)?

NO.The implementation of PGM does not conflict with the practice of employers implementing PLWS provided that employees do not receive a rate less than the minimum wage rate.
